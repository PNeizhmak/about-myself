I lead engineering at Flo Health, the women’s health app with 80M+ monthly users. For five years that meant payments and monetisation. Since September 2026 it means something I find much harder to explain at a dinner party: an AI Delivery tribe, where people and AI agents ship product experiments together

This page is for people who are about to work with me. It’s what I would tell you over the first coffee, if the first coffee were long enough

### 🎯 What I actually do

My job is to make sure our teams turn strategy into results with speed and quality, and to do it in a way we can sustain for years, not quarters. I don’t measure myself by what I personally produce. I measure myself by what we deliver together and by how many people around me grew along the way

Most days I’m a bridge. Between leadership and the teams, between a big goal and the practical path to it, between what product wants and what engineering can promise. I translate company goals into roadmaps people can actually execute, then make sure we track progress with metrics that mean something rather than vanity numbers

The other half of the job is organisational. Hiring, scaling teams across geographies, designing structures where people do their best work and growing the next layer of leaders. If I have a specialty, it’s the last one. Watching an engineer become a Staff Engineer, or a tech lead become a manager who is clearly better at it than I was at their stage, is the part of the job I would do for free

### 🤖 What changed in 2026

For most of my career the hard question was how to get more out of a team of humans. This year the question changed. The tribe I lead has a Delivery Studio team that builds our agent harness, an i18n team and three teams of AI Delivery Engineers who launch experiments, paywalls and content. The people in those teams used to assemble launches by hand. Now the agent does the assembly and they own the judgment: what to launch, whether it’s right, whether it’s safe

A few things I’ve come to believe through this work, mostly by getting them wrong first

Dropping AI tools into an unchanged process just makes the same work faster. The real change is in roles, in who owns what and in what “done” means when an agent did half of it

Guardrails come before autonomy. An agent in our setup runs under the user’s own permissions, produces a draft for a human to approve and leaves a full trace of what it did. Evals are release gates, not a nice-to-have

And none of this works if the people whose jobs are changing don’t trust the person changing them. Most of my time this year went into exactly that, not into prompts

### ⚙️ How I work

Best idea wins. Title doesn’t matter, credibility does, and credibility comes from bringing data rather than gut feelings. I assume you are capable and autonomous by default and I’ll stay out of your way. I step in when you ask, or when the risk is high enough that staying out would be negligence rather than trust

I share context openly and expect the same back. Clarity on goals, trade-offs and constraints is the one thing I’m not flexible about. If you don’t know why we’re doing something, that’s my failure, and I want to hear about it

I balance hands-off trust with deep dives. Sometimes that means I’m in the code, the agent skills or the ADRs myself. I don’t trust technical calls from people who stopped touching the work, and I apply that to myself

I like constructive conflict, as long as it’s rooted in trust and data. And I set a high bar, for myself first

### 🤝 Working with me

Meetings should be short, structured and have a purpose. If it’s a brainstorm, still frame the problem first. I have a low tolerance for rituals that exist because they always existed

If something is urgent, grab me or call. If it isn’t, Slack or a doc works better, I’ll read it properly and come back with a thought-through answer. For strategy, let’s do a 1:1 or a workshop rather than a thread

Direct, candid, fact-based feedback is gold. Give it to me and I’ll give it back. If you escalate something, bring the data, the trade-offs and the options you see. My role is to calibrate the decision, not to take it away from you

In return you can count on me caring about your growth, standing up for engineering and product quality and helping when things get ambiguous or political. What I ask back is simple. Own your problems and bring solutions with them. Be open about blockers and risks. Tell me early when a commitment slips. Keep learning, and share what you learn. I will too

### ✍️ A few personal notes

I love structured problem solving: metric trees, strategy maps, frameworks. I’m happiest with a messy problem and a whiteboard, and least happy in a meeting that is repeating itself

Books and articles are a big part of how I grow, and I’ll keep sending you links. My best energy goes into strategy and scaling teams, and most of all into helping leaders and senior engineers grow

I can solve a Rubik’s Cube, 3x3 and 4x4. The 5x5 is still on the list
